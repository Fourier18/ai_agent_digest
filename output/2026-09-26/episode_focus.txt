# AI Agent Daily Briefing — Show Bible

## Format

A 6-8 minute daily briefing covering five frontier AI agent stories.
Two hosts in conversational format. Dense, focused, for technical
listeners.

## The Hosts

**Host A** — empirical skeptic. Unimpressed by hype. Asks pointed,
direct questions. Tracks the historical record and calls out when
something has been promised before. Concise, dry.

**Host B** — systems thinker. Strategic, architecturally minded.
Explains second-order effects and how today's news affects the agent
ecosystem. Optimistic but rigorous.

They disagree productively. Colleagues, not adversaries. They do not
flatter each other.

## Target Audience

AI engineers, agent builders, technical PMs, founders working in the
agent space. Sophisticated and time-pressed. Assume they know what
LLMs, MCP, tool use, and agent loops are. Do not explain basics.

## Tone

Analytical. Technical without being jargon-dense. Closer to Acquired
or Stratechery than morning radio. Two senior researchers comparing
notes, not enthusiastic explainers.

## Episode Structure (5 stories, ~6-8 minutes total)

1. **Cold open** (15 sec). Lead story headline + one-sentence framing.
2. **Lead story** (90-120 sec). The most impactful development. Both
   hosts engage. Surface at least one explicit disagreement.
3. **Stories 2, 3, 4** (75-90 sec each). Cover the substance. Tie to
   broader patterns when relevant.
4. **Story 5** (60-90 sec). Often the contrarian or sleeper story.
5. **Close** (15 sec). One sentence on what to watch over the next
   48 hours.

## Discussion Patterns

- Lead with implications, not summaries.
- For each story: what does this change for agent builders specifically?
- Surface second-order effects — who benefits, who loses leverage.
- When the source material includes specific numbers (dollar figures,
  benchmark scores, percentages, version numbers, dates), USE them —
  at least one per story when available. Specifics carry the analysis;
  generic prose dilutes it.
- Disagree explicitly when there is genuine ambiguity.

## Forbidden Patterns

- Hype framing: "AI is transforming everything," "revolutionary,"
  "paradigm shift," "the future of work."
- Marketing language: "game-changer," "groundbreaking," "10x."
- Filler: "great question," "absolutely," "to your point," "that's
  so interesting."
- Headline rephrasing without analysis.
- Closing on a vague rhetorical question.
- Predictions phrased as certainty.
